## C. Final scientific stress test

**Psychometrician.** *"A five‑to‑seven‑minute event cannot support a programme‑level competence claim."* — **Already answered.** §8 invokes case specificity (Norman et al., 2006) explicitly; §2.5 maps AIAP to Kane's generalization and extrapolation inferences and concedes AIAP is weakest there; Appendix G makes G‑theory/cross‑classified modelling the *primary* generalization analysis with a decision study; Table 16A names "poor generalization relative to burden" as a falsification condition. **Residual bite:** none that additional design would fix. This is a measurement question awaiting measurement.

*"You will report whichever agreement coefficient looks best."* — **Already answered, unusually well.** The primary score estimand (two‑way random‑effects, absolute‑agreement ICC, single assessor) and the primary decision estimand (raw agreement at the preregistered cut, with full cross‑classification) are fixed in advance; weighted kappa and AC2 are designated *sensitivity* analyses; Pilot Protocol §RQ1 states "No statistic may be selected after outcomes are known." The Vach & Gerke caveat — that they analyse AC1, not AC2 — is stated in the Core, the Appendix, and the Pilot Protocol. This is stronger pre‑specification than most published assessment research.

**Assessment‑security researcher.** *"Monday's student tells Thursday's student the challenge."* — **Detected, contained, and routed**, not merely acknowledged: §7.1.1 requires cohort size, exposure window, allocation rule, use counts, rotation/retirement, exposure indicators, and compromise response; it *prefers* live artifact‑specific perturbation precisely because it cannot circulate; Feasibility Gate G02 blocks the claim if this is ungoverned; the Prompt Bank workbook has an Exposure Log with an "affected cohort/decisions" and "requalification/review" column; the schema records `exposure_window` and `retirement_rule`; the Toolkit FAQ tells students to report circulation. **Residual bite, honestly held by the paper:** detection here is procedural, and the paper says so.

*"Different students get materially different difficulty."* — **Answered.** §7.1.1 requires a common construct blueprint, content and accessibility review, and stakes‑proportionate equivalence evidence; Gate G03 blocks otherwise; the Form Equivalence sheet defaults to "Not assessed" (fail‑closed); the schema's `parallel_form_equivalence.status` includes `not_equivalent`; Standard §6.1 prescribes narrowing, common perturbation, or treating form as uncertainty.

*"Open‑remote authentication is theatre."* — **The paper says so first**, in §10, and prohibits it as a sole high‑consequence warrant absent a modality‑specific threat model. It then does the harder thing: it refuses to declare online‑only provision incapable, and instead enumerates the independent anchors that would suffice. That is the correct treatment.

**Accessibility researcher.** *"Equivalent routes are equivalent in name only."* — **Answered better than most frameworks.** Appendix D3 provides a worked criterion‑by‑criterion mapping across all five competence components with named equivalence safeguards, and then states plainly: "An alternative route is not equivalent merely because it is easier to schedule." Construct equivalence is explicitly held open as an empirical question with pilot comparisons of score distributions, decision consistency, burden, and subgroup effects. **Residual bite:** equivalence remains unproven — correctly flagged, not concealed.

**Academic‑integrity scholar.** *"Lane 2A lets a student who outsourced production pass."* — **Conceded without euphemism** in §7.2: "Lane 2A does not solve the production problem; it changes what is certified." This is the paper's most intellectually honest move and it is load‑bearing for everything downstream, including the public‑claim accounting rule.

**Institutional leader.** *"We cannot afford this."* — **Answered as a validity condition, not a complaint.** The Capacity Gate makes unaffordability a *blocker on the claim*, not a reason to dilute evidence; the capacity‑degradation rule closes the mid‑cycle loophole; the workload model separates one‑off from recurring and staff‑hours from elapsed time. The multiplier finding (recurring burden ≈ 3.6× / 2.7× nominal live hours) is the honest number, published rather than buried.

**Student advocate.** *"This is surveillance and interpretive load transfer."* — **Answered, and self‑applied.** §3 engages Gonsalves (2026) directly and sets a legitimacy test AIAP can *fail*: "AIAP fails this legitimacy test if student‑facing instructions grow long, records become self‑incriminating, or designer‑side complexity is transferred to students." Data minimization is default; AI‑use records are required only for a declared purpose; the least‑intrusive‑control rule governs proctoring; Box 5 and the Toolkit keep student‑facing text short. **Residual bite:** whether load actually falls is a hypothesis, and is labelled as one.

**Accreditor.** *"What exactly does the degree certify?"* — **This is AIAP's strongest single contribution.** §7.7, Table 9, the assurance‑composition rule, and Figure 4's forbidden inferential transfers give an accreditor something no adjacent framework supplies: an auditable statement of which proportion of decision weight rests on which kind of warrant, with explicit prohibition on treating a larger share as automatically better.

**Statistician.** *"A feasibility cohort of \~120 cannot carry eight co‑primary estimands."* — **Anticipated and disarmed.** Appendix G demotes eight questions to secondary/exploratory precisely on this ground, requires sample size to be derived from the primary estimand and precision target, requires projected *and* achieved subgroup counts, and states three times that non‑significance is not equivalence. Correct.

## The two substantive gaps

**No minimum applicable-dimension rule.** §6.1 requires "a common set of applicable dimensions" and warns that "a reviewer may not remove a weak dimension strategically through `not_applicable`" — but it names the motive without supplying a structural floor. Your own §11.4 arithmetic shows why this matters: P₅(d) rises from 0.44% at d = 12 to 3.36% at d = 8 to 25.6% at d = 4 to 80% at d = 1.
